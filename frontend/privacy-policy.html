1. Overview & Scope

The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal — ForenSync Cyber Portal (“Portal”, “We”, “Us”) is an official law-enforcement digital infrastructure operated under the Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India. The Portal enables citizens to:

  • File cyber crime complaints electronically
  • Track complaint status via unique Ticket IDs
  • Upload digital forensic evidence for ongoing investigations
  • Receive public advisory notifications regarding cyber threats

This Privacy Policy applies to all natural persons (“Data Principals”) who interact with this Portal, including citizens filing complaints, witnesses uploading evidence, and law-enforcement personnel operating the internal investigator workstation. It does not apply to third-party websites linked from this Portal.

Statutory Authority: This Portal is established under powers vested by Sections 66C, 66D, and 78 of the Information Technology Act, 2000, read with Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (for FIR registration). Use of this Portal constitutes acceptance of this Policy.

3. Purpose of Data Collection

Personal data is used exclusively for the following specified and lawful purposes:

  1. Complaint Registration & Ticketing: Generate a Ticket ID, route the complaint to the appropriate desk, and notify the complainant.
  2. Active Investigation: Enable investigators to review, annotate, prioritise, and resolve complaints within their designated jurisdiction.
  3. Forensic Evidence Preservation: Securely store uploaded evidence with SHA-256 integrity hashes and a legally admissible chain-of-custody audit trail.
  4. AI-Assisted Routing: Keyword-based NLP auto-routes complaints and assigns priority scores. No individual profiling is performed.
  5. FIR & Legal Proceedings: Auto-generate structured FIR draft documents for official filing under CrPC Section 154.
  6. Crime Analytics: Aggregated, anonymised statistics for internal crime trend analysis and resource allocation only. No individual-level data is published.
  7. Portal Security: CAPTCHA verification and session management to prevent abuse and unauthorised access.
Purpose Limitation: Data collected for a specific purpose will not be used for any other purpose without a fresh lawful basis, in line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act).

5. Data Storage & Security Measures

We implement “Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures” as mandated by Rule 8 of the IT (Reasonable Security Practices) Rules, 2011. Safeguards include:

  • Transport Encryption: All data in transit is encrypted using TLS 1.3. Plain HTTP is auto-redirected to HTTPS.
  • Password Storage: Employee passwords are bcrypt-hashed (salt-factor 12+). Plaintext passwords are never stored.
  • OTP Expiry: Email OTPs expire within 10 minutes and are single-use to prevent replay attacks.
  • Evidence Integrity: SHA-256 hash is computed at upload and re-verified on every access to detect tampering.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Investigators access only cases assigned to their desk. Admin privileges are separately provisioned.
  • Audit Logging: All evidence access, status changes, and FIR filings are timestamped and logged immutably.
  • CAPTCHA: Canvas-rendered CAPTCHA prevents automated abuse of the complaint submission endpoint.
  • Data Storage: The backend database resides on government-controlled secure server infrastructure.
No System is Infallible: In the event of a data breach affecting your rights, we will notify affected data principals and the designated authority under the DPDP Act, 2023, within the prescribed timeframe.

6. Data Sharing & Disclosure

Citizen data is treated as strictly confidential and is not sold, rented, or traded. Data may be shared only in the following circumstances:

  1. Law Enforcement Agencies: Data may be shared with other police units, the CBI, the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in), or CERT-In as required for investigation or pursuant to a lawful order.
  2. Courts & Judiciary: Evidence files, audit trails, and FIR drafts may be produced before courts of competent jurisdiction in India as required under CrPC.
  3. Government Ministries / Departments: Aggregated, anonymised crime statistics may be shared with the Ministry of Home Affairs, NITI Aayog, or the State Home Department for policy purposes only.
  4. International Cooperation: Where crimes involve foreign actors, relevant data may be shared through established MLAT channels, subject to Central Government approval.
  5. OTP Email Service: Your email address is transmitted to an approved SMTP provider solely for OTP delivery. This service does not receive any complaint or case data.
No Commercial Sharing: Data submitted through this Portal will never be shared with private companies, advertisers, data brokers, or any entity for commercial purposes.

7. Digital Evidence Handling & Chain of Custody

This Portal employs a forensically sound digital evidence workflow aligned with Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, and the guidelines issued by CERT-In and NFSU (Gandhinagar).

  • Upon upload, each evidence file is assigned a unique exhibit reference and a SHA-256 hash is stored as the baseline integrity fingerprint.
  • Every subsequent access generates an immutable chain-of-custody log entry recording: actor identity, UTC timestamp, and action performed.
  • Any hash mismatch on re-computation triggers an automatic tamper alert and flags the evidence as potentially compromised.
  • Evidence files are stored in a dedicated, access-controlled directory, not publicly accessible via URL.
  • All evidence handling by investigators is governed by the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for digital forensics.
Forensic Integrity: The SHA-256 chain-of-custody mechanism ensures digital evidence remains admissible and unimpeachable in Indian courts as per the Section 65B certification requirements.

9. Your Rights Under the DPDP Act, 2023

As a Data Principal under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you have the following rights:

  • Right to Access (§11): Request a summary of personal data we hold about you and how it has been processed.
  • Right to Correction and Erasure (§12): Request correction of inaccurate data. Erasure requests will be honoured where there is no statutory reason to retain data (e.g., active investigation or court order).
  • Right to Grievance Redressal (§13): Have your grievances addressed by our Grievance Officer within the prescribed timelines. See Section 14.
  • Right to Nominate (§14): Nominate another individual to exercise your rights in the event of your death or incapacity.
  • Right to Withdraw Consent (§6): Where processing is based on consent (e.g., OTP verification), you may withdraw consent. This may affect the Portal’s ability to process your complaint.
Limitations: Pursuant to Schedule II of the DPDP Act, 2023, your rights may be limited where processing is for: prevention or detection of crime; prosecution of an offence; national security; or judicial/quasi-judicial proceedings.

Requests will be acknowledged within 3 working days and resolved within 30 days. Contact the Grievance Officer at Section 14.

11. Children’s Privacy & Protection

This Portal acknowledges its special obligations regarding children (persons below 18 years) under the DPDP Act, 2023 (§9), POCSO Act, 2012, and Juvenile Justice Act, 2015.

  • Complaints involving cyber crimes against children (CSAM, online grooming, cyberbullying) are automatically routed to the Women & Child Cyber Cases Desk with elevated priority.
  • All data relating to child victims is treated as strictly confidential and access is restricted to designated specialist investigators only.
  • The identity of child victims is never disclosed in any public-facing communication, analytics report, or to parties not legally authorised under POCSO.
  • Where a child (or guardian on their behalf) submits a complaint, processing is deemed to be with verifiable parental/guardian consent per §9(1) of the DPDP Act.
  • This Portal does not profile, target, or collect data from children for any purpose other than investigating crimes committed against or by them.

14. Grievance Redressal Mechanism

In accordance with Rule 5(9) of the IT (Intermediaries Guidelines) Rules, 2021, and Section 13 of the DPDP Act, 2023, a Grievance Officer has been appointed:

Grievance Resolution Process:

1. Submit your grievance in writing (email or post) to the Grievance Officer (see Section 15).
2. Acknowledgement within 3 working days.
3. Resolution communicated within 30 calendar days.
4. If unsatisfied, you may escalate to the Appellate Authority designated under the DPDP Act, 2023, or approach a court of competent jurisdiction.

For complaints regarding cyber crimes (not portal privacy), please use the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call the National Helpline 1930.
